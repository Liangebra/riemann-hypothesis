## Common comparison domain

Let F be a number field, l a prime, r>0, R=Z/l^rZ, M a finite discrete R-module, and S a finite set of finite places containing those over l. In the literature write S' = S union the archimedean places. The order m of M is an l-power and is a unit outside S'. The project [Global.lean](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/a28ad7ec8b9f5ff4ec448fe210d3ac57aacfffe1/src/ReciprocityTheory/GaloisValuations/Global.lean#L183-L195) defines NS as the closed normal subgroup generated by inertia outside S, and GS as the quotient of the absolute Galois group by NS. By the usual Galois correspondence this is the maximal extension unramified outside S', not a quotient of the local cohomology groups. The identification of Dec with the absolute Galois group of the completion is the standard henselization/completion comparison; the project's bridge implementing it has not yet been inspected. No fresh elaboration or kernel-dependency export is claimed.

## GC-004 — global finiteness

[GlobalFinitenessAt](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/a28ad7ec8b9f5ff4ec448fe210d3ac57aacfffe1/src/ReciprocityTheory/Hypotheses/GalCoh.lean#L132-L136) asserts finiteness in degrees 0,1,2 and vanishing in degrees at least 3 when l is odd. Milne, Arithmetic Duality Theorems (2006), [I.4.15, p.62](https://wstein.org/edu/2010/582e/refs/milne-arithmetic_duality_theorems.pdf#page=70) proves finiteness in every degree for finite S', and [I.4.10(c), p.57](https://wstein.org/edu/2010/582e/refs/milne-arithmetic_duality_theorems.pdf#page=65) identifies higher cohomology with that at real places. For an odd-primary module, 2 is invertible on M. The cohomology of a group of order two vanishes in positive degrees by averaging, hence so does global H^n for n>=3. The project does not assert higher finiteness for l=2. It also does not claim higher vanishing for arbitrary 2-primary modules. The restriction is mathematically appropriate. Candidate narrowMatch.

## GC-005 — global Euler characteristic

[GlobalEulerCharAt and archH0Card](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/a28ad7ec8b9f5ff4ec448fe210d3ac57aacfffe1/src/ReciprocityTheory/Hypotheses/GalCoh.lean#L125-L143) correspond to Milne [I.5.1, p.67 and footnote 13](https://wstein.org/edu/2010/582e/refs/milne-arithmetic_duality_theorems.pdf#page=75). Put h_i=|H^i(GS,M)|; let r1, r2 be the counts of real and complex places. The classical expression is

    h_0 h_2 / h_1 = (product over real w of |M^{c_w}| / m) * (product over complex w of m / m^2).

Thus h_0 h_2 m^{r1+2r2} = h_1 (product over real w of |M^{c_w}|) m^{r2}.

Because [F:Q]=r1+2r2, this is exactly the arithmetic identity of the Lean interface. The project defines IsCCAt via an embedding of the algebraic closure into C whose restriction represents w, and conjugation by c corresponds to complex conjugation ([GalCohDefs.lean](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/a28ad7ec8b9f5ff4ec448fe210d3ac57aacfffe1/src/ReciprocityTheory/Hypotheses/GalCohDefs.lean)). The denominator m^2 at complex places comes from the normalized absolute value, not from cohomology. Ordinary H0, not Tate-modified H0, occurs in this formula: Milne footnote 13 explicitly emphasizes that distinction for 2-primary coefficients. Therefore the absence of l≠2 from GlobalEulerCharAt is not a defect. Candidate narrowMatch relative to the broader classical formula.

## GC-006 — Greenberg–Wiles

[GreenbergWilesAt](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/a28ad7ec8b9f5ff4ec448fe210d3ac57aacfffe1/src/ReciprocityTheory/Hypotheses/GalCoh.lean#L156-L169). Wiles, Modular elliptic curves and Fermat's Last Theorem, Annals of Mathematics 141 (1995), 443–551, [Prop. 1.6, p.472](https://www.williamstein.org/edu/Fall2003/252/references/wiles-flt/Wiles-Modular_Elliptic_Curves_and_Fermats_Last_Theorem.pdf), gives the historical form over Q for finite odd-primary coefficients, with local factors expressed using indices. Do not identify that statement alone as a general-number-field reference. For a general number field and finite Galois module, the explicit ratio appears in [Galois cohomology and the Greenberg–Wiles formula, Thm. 1.8, p.5](https://www.asmith-math.org/CRMnotes.pdf#page=5). The latter is a lecture-note source, not a claim of original publication.

Write D=M^D, L_v for the selected local subgroup, L_v^perp for the annihilator, and s(M,L) for the size of the Selmer group. The general ratio is

    s(M,L)/s(D,L^perp) = |H^0(F,M)|/|H^0(F,D)| * product_all_v (|L_v|/|H^0(F_v,M)|).

For finite v outside S, set L_v=H^1_ur(F_v,M). Inflation-restriction identifies this with H^1(procyclic quotient,M). For a finite unramified module its order equals |M^{Frob_v}| by counting kernel and cokernel of Frob_v−1. Since v does not divide l, unramified orthogonality makes the dual condition unramified as well. The factors are one. For real and complex places positive-degree cohomology is zero because l is odd; the numerator is one and the product of denominators is archH0Card. Restricting to GS is legitimate for both modules: a global cocycle with trivial inertia restrictions outside S descends to GS since the action there is trivial and its cocycle vanishes on the closed normal subgroup generated by those inertia groups. Conversely inflation yields these local conditions. This also uses the cyclotomic twist factoring through GS, explicitly required by muS.comp (toGS Ω _) = cycMod.

Cross multiplication gives

    s(M,L) |H^0(F,D)| (product_v in S |H^0(F_v,M)|) archH0Card
      = s(D,L^perp) |H^0(F,M)| (product_v in S |L_v|).

This is precisely the shape of the formal equality. In [ContinuousConstSMulQuotient.lean](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/a28ad7ec8b9f5ff4ec448fe210d3ac57aacfffe1/src/ReciprocityTheory/GaloisValuations/ContinuousConstSMulQuotient.lean#L205-L216), Sel is the intersection of inverse images of L_v under restriction. In GalCohDefs, perpCond is the intersection of kernels of evaluation under the cup map; it matches the annihilator, up to the dual-first order that does not affect orthogonality. A local submodule over Z/l^rZ is equivalent to an additive subgroup of the underlying module since integer multiples preserve every additive subgroup. Finiteness is needed before dividing cardinalities. The comparison is not an equivalence proof in Lean. Candidate narrowMatch with a generalized reference; historical Wiles is not the sole source.

## GC-007 — unramified local orthogonality

[UnramifiedOrthogonalityAt](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/a28ad7ec8b9f5ff4ec448fe210d3ac57aacfffe1/src/ReciprocityTheory/Hypotheses/GalCoh.lean#L80-L96) requires v not over l and trivial action of inertia on M. The theorem asserts both annihilator descriptions. Compare the local duality/orthogonality statement [Smith lecture notes, Thm. 1.3](https://www.asmith-math.org/CRMnotes.pdf#page=4). The finite l-primary dual is likewise unramified since the l^r-th roots of unity are unramified at v not dividing l. The formal H1ur is the kernel of restriction to the inertia subgroup, [ContinuousConstSMulQuotient.lean](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/a28ad7ec8b9f5ff4ec448fe210d3ac57aacfffe1/src/ReciprocityTheory/GaloisValuations/ContinuousConstSMulQuotient.lean#L220-L226); inflation-restriction supplies the usual description. The opposite cup order does not change the zero relation. Candidate narrowMatch, contingent on the decomposition and inertia comparison; a precise published primary reference remains desirable.

## GC-008 — cardinality fragment of Poitou–Tate

[ShaDualityAt](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/a28ad7ec8b9f5ff4ec448fe210d3ac57aacfffe1/src/ReciprocityTheory/Hypotheses/GalCoh.lean#L145-L154) asserts that the two localization kernels Sha1(M), Sha2(D) are finite and have equal cardinality. [Milne I.4.10(a)](https://wstein.org/edu/2010/582e/refs/milne-arithmetic_duality_theorems.pdf#page=65) supplies a canonical perfect pairing. For odd l the positive-degree archimedean terms vanish, so the kernels at S alone agree with the kernels at S'. A perfect pairing into Q/Z gives equality of finite orders. The converse is not a claim of the interface: two finite abelian groups of order l^2, Z/l^2 and (Z/l)^2, have equal order but are not dual to one another. This example separates abstract statements, not actual arithmetic Sha groups. Candidate hasPart (the formal result is a numerical consequence of a part of the source theorem, not a formalization of the canonical pairing). Do not confuse this interface with the full normalized Poitou–Tate theorem elsewhere in the project.
